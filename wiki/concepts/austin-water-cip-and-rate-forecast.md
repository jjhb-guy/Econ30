**Austin Water** forecasts operating finances for water, wastewater, reclaimed water, and related services. The FY 2026–2030 city forecast emphasizes that results are **primarily driven by growth in Austin Water’s Capital Improvement Program (CIP)**, including **cash transfers to CIP** and **scheduled debt service**, in an environment of **rising construction costs** and ongoing **rehabilitation and replacement** needs.

## Revenue

- **FY 2026** operating revenue is projected up about **8.1%** versus FY 2025 budget, mostly from **service rate** increases and customer growth, partially offset by lower **interest income**.
- Through **FY 2030**, operating revenue growth versus FY 2026 is dominated by **rate increases** and modest **customer growth**.

## Typical residential bill

- The typical residential customer (**5,800 gallons water** and **4,000 gallons wastewater** per month) is shown at **$100.15/month in FY 2026**, up about **9.8%** from FY 2025, with further monthly increases through FY 2030 that cumulatively reach about **37.5%** above the FY 2026 level in the narrative’s cumulative figure.

## Expenditures and staffing

- **FY 2026** expenditure growth (about **7.5%** vs FY 2025 requirements) is driven by **CIP cash transfers** and **higher debt service**, plus citywide wage, insurance, retention stipends, commodities/contracts, and **13** new positions converted from temporary status.
- Through **FY 2030**, the forecast includes large incremental **CIP transfers** and **debt service**, plus **88** cumulative new positions across support, operations, engineering/quality, and customer experience.

## Plan vs forecast

- The document states **FY 2026 Forecast revenue** is nearly aligned with the **FY 2026 Plan** from the prior budget cycle, while **FY 2026 Forecast expenditures** are **lower** than the FY 2026 Plan year, explained partly by **debt service** and **reclaimed-water CIP transfer** adjustments and a slower **FTE ramp** (fewer positions in FY 2026, more in out-years).
