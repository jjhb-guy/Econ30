The City of Austin **General Fund** is the principal operating fund for core municipal services. The FY 2026–2030 financial forecast describes **expenditure growth**, **revenue composition**, and a **multi-year gap** between recurring revenues and expenditures under its baseline assumptions.

## Expenditure mix and FY 2026 drivers

- **FY 2026** base General Fund expenditures are projected at about **$1.5 billion**, roughly **$76.6 million (5.4%)** above the FY 2025 Amended Budget.
- Roughly **two-thirds** of the General Fund supports **public safety** (Police, Fire, EMS, Forensic Science); **community service** departments account for most of the remainder, with **transfers and other requirements** making up a small share.
- Notable FY 2026 cost drivers in the narrative include **sworn public safety compensation** (including labor agreements and placeholders for Fire negotiations), **civilian personnel and benefits**, **allocated citywide support and IT/fleet charges**, **Homelessness Strategy Office** costs including the **8th Street shelter**, **new Fire ladder staffing**, and a net **Central Booking / Municipal Court** adjustment tied to a **Travis County** interlocal arrangement.

## Five-year expenditure trajectory

- From FY 2026 through FY 2030, General Fund expenditures are projected to rise by an additional **$248.4 million**, implying about a **3.9% compound annual growth rate**, with large components attributed to **personnel-related costs**, **allocated citywide costs**, and **transfers** to other funds.

## Revenue and structural balance

- Revenue categories include **property tax**, **sales tax**, **utility transfers in**, and **other revenue** (fees, fines, interest, etc.).
- The forecast incorporates a **material decline in taxable property value** in FY 2026 based on **preliminary Travis Central Appraisal District** indications, with **moderate** value growth assumed thereafter and **lower new-construction value** than recent peak years.
- **Sales tax** growth is projected to be **weak** in the near term, with higher growth rates only later in the horizon.
- The fund summary table in the source shows **revenues and transfers in** trailing **expenditures and transfers out** in FY 2026–FY 2030, producing **widening deficits** on that presentation, under the note that projections **assume the voter-approval tax rate** each year. **Typical homeowner monthly property tax** (for the illustrated homeowner) rises from **$164.13** to **$219.16** over FY 2025–FY 2030 in that table.

## Management and vacancy context

- The document notes **FY 2025** ending **close to amended budget** for expenditures, with **little vacancy savings** expected amid hiring progress, and describes a **vacancy policy** (positions vacant beyond two years eliminated; departments above **15%** vacancy restricted from new position requests) repeated for FY 2026 planning.
