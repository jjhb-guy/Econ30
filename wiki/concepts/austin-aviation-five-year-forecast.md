Austin-Bergstrom International Airport (AUS) is modeled as an enterprise operation funded by airport revenues, with the FY 2026-FY 2030 forecast driven by expansion-phase debt and operating scale-up.

## Main drivers

- The forecast ties long-run growth to the Airport Expansion and Development Program.
- A new airline use and lease agreement beginning in FY 2026 is a central revenue driver relative to the prior methodology.

## Revenue outlook

- FY 2026 operating revenue is projected to increase by **$83.9 million (+23.9%)** versus FY 2025 budget, mainly from airline revenue under the new agreement.
- By FY 2030, operating revenue is projected to rise by **$292.6 million (+67.3%)** versus FY 2026, with growth in both airline and non-airline categories.
- Fund table revenue values (in millions): **$434.9 (FY26)** to **$727.5 (FY30)**.

## Expenditures and staffing

- FY 2026 operating expenditures are projected up **$6.9 million (+2.5%)** versus FY 2025, including new custodial/security/grounds positions and citywide compensation effects.
- By FY 2030, expenditures are projected up an additional **$256.1 million (+88.4%)** versus FY 2026, largely due to debt service and expanded facility operations.
- FTEs increase from **694 (FY26)** to **764 (FY30)** in the table.

## Transfer structure

- The fund summary reflects substantial transfers to capital and major maintenance funds as growth proceeds.
- Transfer to Airport Capital Fund is shown at **$115.1 million (FY26)** and **$147.2 million (FY30)**; transfer to Major Maintenance Fund appears from FY 2026 onward (**$30.0 million** in FY26 to **$34.4 million** in FY30).
