The Watershed Protection Department (WPD) forecast is driven by planned drainage utility fee increases, citywide cost pressures, and staffing growth tied to service demand and strategic planning.

## Revenue outlook

- FY 2026 operating revenue is projected up **$4.5 million (+3.8%)** versus FY 2025 budget, mostly from drainage utility charge increases.
- By FY 2030, revenue is projected up **$21.8 million (+17.6%)** versus FY 2026, with fee increases partly offset by lower projected interest income.

## Typical drainage bill

- Typical residential drainage customer is modeled at **$13.94/month in FY 2026**, up **$0.56 (+4.2%)** from FY 2025.
- Published bill path: **$13.38, $13.94, $14.64, $15.37, $16.14, $16.95** (FY25-FY30).

## Expenditures and personnel

- FY 2026 expenditures are projected up **$3.7 million (+3.0%)** versus FY 2025 budget, including citywide base costs and added positions, partly offset by lower planned infrastructure transfers.
- By FY 2030, expenditures are projected up an additional **$22.7 million (+18.6%)** versus FY 2026.
- The forecast references **44 additional positions** over out-years, with alignment to the Rain to River strategic plan to be reviewed annually.

## Fund summary

- Fund table (in millions): revenue/transfers in **$124.1 (FY26)** to **$145.9 (FY30)**; expenditures/transfers out **$126.1 (FY26)** to **$145.1 (FY30)**.
- Ending fund balance is shown at **$10.0 million** by FY 2030.
